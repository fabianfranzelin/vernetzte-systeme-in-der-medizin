Motivation

The development of safety-critical networked software follows the same principles in automotive and medical devices, but codifies them in different standards. This document maps the two worlds concept-by-concept.

Standard-by-standard comparison

Concern Automotive Medical
Service-oriented comms SOME/IP (AUTOSAR AP) SDC (IEEE 11073-20701)
Real-time streaming DDS, iceoryx (zero-copy) DDS, SDC waveform states
Software safety ISO 26262 (part 6, ASIL A-D) IEC 62304 (Class A/B/C)
System safety ISO 26262 (full) IEC 60601-1 + IEC 62304 + ISO 14971
Risk management ISO 26262 part 3 (HARA) ISO 14971 (standalone)
Cybersecurity ISO/SAE 21434 IEC 62443 + MDR + FDA guidance
Process maturity Automotive SPICE ISO 13485 + IEC 62304
Interoperability testing AUTOSAR conformance IHE Connectathons, OR.NET testevents

What transfers

  • Architectural patterns: service-oriented communication, publish/subscribe, QoS-driven streaming, safety cases, hazard-analysis discipline.
  • Process discipline: traceability from stakeholder requirement to test case; independent verification and validation; configuration management.
  • Test infrastructure: cloud-based V&V farms, digital twins, MIL/SIL/HIL.

What does not transfer

  • Domain semantics. BICEPS models patients and clinical workflows; CAN/Ethernet frames do not. The information model is the problem.
  • Clinical benefit in the risk equation. ISO 14971 explicitly weighs residual risk against clinical benefit. Automotive does not.
  • Human factors emphasis. IEC 62366 (usability) has no direct automotive analogue at the same regulatory weight.

The core insight

Architectural patterns are transferable across safety-critical domains. Domain semantics — and the way they enter the safety case — are not.

This is where the interesting engineering happens, and why cross-domain expertise is undervalued and underused.
